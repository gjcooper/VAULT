## My Thoughts

The overall thesis for this paper is that people can infer other peoples preferences (and preference strength) from their choices and response times. Faster response times lead people to infer a larger preference difference between the chosen and unchosen option.

They reference prior work that also discusses this, however they formalise this by creating an inverse model of the [[diffusion decision model]]. They follow their qualitative simulation work with four experiments, showing inference of preferences, inference over previously unseen choices as well as the ability to take the other persons motivations (or engagement with the choices) into consideration when inferring about their choice RT.

This paper could act as an inverse point to my work on the [[Bayesian-Belief response time model]], as this work looks into inferring peoples preferences/uncertainty based on response times, whereas my work looks at a persons response times based on uncertainty about anothers beliefs.
