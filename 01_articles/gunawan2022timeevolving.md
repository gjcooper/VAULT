# Time-evolving psychological processes over repeated decisions

Gunawan, D., Hawkins, G. E., Kohn, R., Tran, M.-N., & Brown, S. D. (2022). Time-evolving psychological processes over repeated decisions. _Psychological Review_, _129_(3), 438–456. [https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000351](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000351)
[online](http://zotero.org/users/7162438/items/4EXKA7XF) [local](zotero://select/library/items/4EXKA7XF) [pdf](file:///home/gjc216/Zotero/storage/JJ6CIFLP/2022-74641-002.pdf)

## My Thoughts

This paper outlines the work from [[Guy E. Hawkins|Guy Hawkins]] and [[David Gunawan]] that takes fairly simple decision making tasks and asks the question of how to model in a principled way the fact that peoples performce (and associated underlying processing) is unlikely to be static over the timeframe of the experiment.

In order to do this they introduce three models, one with an [[autoregressive]] process, one with smooth polynomial trends and finally a third with a switching trial by trial [[hidden Markov model]].

The Markov model provides a good account of the data. This may be a useful paper to revisit if I am looking at data that shows distinct states or changes in time of performance/outcome measures.
